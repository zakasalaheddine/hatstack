# /ceo-plan — business strategy plan

Plan a strategic decision as three genuinely distinct paths, then stop for a human decision. Use the **three-approaches** skill. Strategy is where the "picked-it-in-my-head-then-argued-for-it" failure does the most damage — the alternatives must be real, not strawmen.

## Interrogate first

- What decision actually has to be made, and by when? A concrete fork, not "improve the business".
- What is the goal in one number, and over what horizon?
- What are the hard constraints — cash runway, headcount, a board mandate, a regulatory line?
- What does the org have to believe for each path to work, and which of those beliefs is least certain?
- What has been tried, and what did the market say?

## Write the plan

Write `.hatstack/plans/<slug>.md`, record its path in `.hatstack/active-plan`. Three `## Approach N` sections that differ in *direction*, not in tactics — e.g. *go up-market to enterprise* vs *widen the self-serve funnel* vs *hold and extend runway*. Each needs **Pros**, **Cons**, **Reversibility**, **Cost**. For strategy, name the *bet* each path makes and the single assumption that, if wrong, sinks it.

The `## Success criteria` section gates strategic deliverables (a memo, a model, an OKR set) and the reviewer verifies each:

```markdown
## Success criteria
- [ ] one measurable goal with a horizon and a leading indicator to watch
- [ ] each path's core assumption stated and the cheapest test of it identified
- [ ] a pre-committed decision point: what evidence, by when, would reverse the choice
```

Reversibility is the tiebreaker — a reversible strategy converts a bet into an experiment. Present the paths to the human and stop. Write `APPROVED:` only when they answer.
